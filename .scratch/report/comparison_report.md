# Street Legal Racing: Redline — video-only fidelity audit

## 1. Scope and overall finding

**A demonstrates a materially different experience from the strongest build-606 reference footage in several core workflows and in its visual presentation.** The largest demonstrated differences concern starting a career, acquiring a car, using the workshop and catalogue, entering races, and returning from them. The car models, workshop, road environments, instruments, and menus also differ substantially.

A does demonstrate working interactions: purchases change balances and ownership, selected upgrades and tuning values change their displayed state, repairs change condition indicators, paint changes the car’s appearance, and a scripted rookie race reaches a defeat screen and pays $53. These deserve credit. They do not establish equivalence to the corresponding original workflows. [A 00:01:27–00:01:38; A 00:03:18–00:03:59; A 00:04:12–00:04:42; A 00:08:22–00:09:03]

One particularly clear functional problem is **the selected forward/“hood” view blocking the road with opaque surfaces**. It occurs with the daytime truck and again with the nighttime Duhen. Other camera modes restore a usable road view. The observation is strong; the cause cannot be determined from video. [A 00:06:46–00:07:04; A 00:10:07–00:10:16]

This judgment applies **only to the retired playable application shown in A**. Nothing here establishes the state of the separate, unshown exact-port effort.

### Principal limits

- **Inspection was sampled, not continuous audiovisual playback.** I inspected a frame from every one of A’s 179 listed chapters, including its final frame, and inspected denser chronological sequences around consequential actions. Every reference received beginning, middle, and end sampling; B1 and B2 received substantially deeper inspection.
- **A has no audio track.** All reference files contain audio streams, but I did not audition them. There are no audio-fidelity findings.
- **B1 and B2 visibly identify themselves as v2.2.1/build 606.** They provide the strongest available baseline. Their videos cannot certify an unmodified installation. Several other references explicitly show mods or another release.
- Scripted inputs, seeded funds/condition/reputation, and directly staged outcomes are separated below. A staged state can demonstrate its rendering and subsequent transaction, but does not establish natural progression, a legitimately earned win, or complete race execution.
- The review does not establish hidden implementation details, numerical simulation equivalence, asset provenance, save-file compatibility, human input latency, or real-time performance.

No overall completion percentage is warranted.

## 4. Detailed gap register

**Priority describes fidelity impact, not estimated implementation effort.** “Critical” means a central player loop differs or remains unverified; it does not necessarily mean a crash.

Observation confidence and stock-baseline confidence are separate. **High baseline confidence** means corroboration in the two visibly build-606 references; **medium** generally means one such source or a material scenario/configuration limitation. Neither certifies the installation’s files.

### Confirmed workflow and presentation differences

#### G001 — Main menu identity and navigation surface  
**Major · Observed mismatch · Observation: high · Baseline: high**

The original’s shown menu uses a blurred car background, framed two-column layout, italic lettering, and entries for career operations, car operations, free ride, quickrace, options, and other modes. A presents its own black/red title screen with Continue, New Game, and Quit. [B1 00:00:05–00:00:10; B2 00:00:00–00:00:10; A 00:00:10–00:00:15; A 00:05:54–00:05:58]

The displayed entry point, information hierarchy, and available navigation surface differ. This does **not** establish that every unlisted mode is absent elsewhere.

**Acceptance demonstration:** show the target’s fresh and existing-career menu states at a matched aspect ratio, navigate the same enabled/disabled entries, and return from one submenu. Compare labels, layout, focus/selection, and transitions.

#### G002 — Career creation grants a different initial situation  
**Critical · Observed mismatch · Observation: high · Baseline: high**

B1 shows name and portrait selection; B1 and B2 then show an empty garage with $20,000 and a direction to purchase a used car. A’s New Game immediately grants a worn Duhen and $2,500. This changes the first ownership decision, initial spending constraints, onboarding, and reason to visit the dealer.

**Acceptance demonstration:** start a clean career in both implementations, complete the same profile choices, and capture the first unobscured garage state and dealer entry. Initial car ownership, displayed funds, introductory feedback, and available next actions must agree.
